The problem

School networks weren't built for esports.

Most event organizers are teachers, coaches or club volunteers. They shouldn't have to be network engineers too.

Lag and disconnects

Content filters, shared Wi-Fi and a crowd of spectator phones fight players for bandwidth in the middle of a match.

Cheating is hard to spot

Aimbots, macro tools, account sharing and remote "coaching" are invisible from the referee's table.

IT says no

District IT can't open ports or loosen filters for one weekend, and shouldn't have to.

What we do

Two services, one crew.

Book both, or just the one you need.

Network

Secure event network setup

A self-contained network we bring in, plug into your uplink, and take away afterward.

  • Wired player stations. Every competitor on Ethernet, with cable runs, switches and spares supplied.
  • Segmented VLANs. Players, staff, stream and guests kept apart so one can't affect the others.
  • Gaming QoS. Match traffic first, stream upload second, everything else after.
  • Allowlist firewall. Player machines reach only game servers, launchers and the tournament platform.
  • Backup uplink. Optional 5G failover so a dropped school line doesn't stop the final.
  • DDoS-aware setup. No public IPs exposed for player machines, and rate limits on the guest side.
Integrity

Anti-cheat monitoring

Layered checks that sit on top of each game's own anti-cheat, plus a person watching the console.

  • Station lockdown. Clean, imaged setups or a check of school PCs for unapproved software, USB devices and macro tools.
  • Traffic monitoring. Flags connections to known cheat services, VPNs, remote-desktop tools and unexpected hosts.
  • Login verification. Matches each in-game account to the registered player and station.
  • Live integrity console. Station health, ping and alerts on one screen for your referee.
  • Replay and log retention. Evidence kept for 30 days to support disputes and appeals.
  • Post-event report. Plain-language summary of every flag and how it was resolved.

    FAQ

    Questions schools ask us

    Do you change our school's network?

    No. We connect to a single uplink port your IT team provides and run everything behind our own firewall. When we leave, your network is exactly as it was.

    What data do you collect about students?

    Only what's needed for integrity: station ID, in-game account name, network connection logs and game replays. We don't install software on personal devices, and we don't collect keystrokes, screen recordings or webcam footage. Logs are deleted after 30 days unless a dispute is open. We provide a data summary you can share with parents.

    Does this replace the game's own anti-cheat?

    No, it adds to it. Game anti-cheat (Vanguard, Easy Anti-Cheat and so on) watches the client. We watch the network, the station setup and who is actually playing, which catches things like VPN routing, remote-access tools, macro hardware and account sharing.

    What happens when something is flagged?

    The tech checks the alert, then tells your referee what they found. Rulings stay with you and your league. We provide the evidence, not the verdict.

    Can you use our existing school computers?

    Yes. We run a pre-event check for unapproved software and devices, and can apply a temporary event profile. If your machines can't run the game, we can bring loaner stations.

    Do you work with leagues like PlayVS or NASEF?

    We set up networks to meet the connection and allowlist needs of the platforms your league uses, and work alongside their rules and reporting.
